What the Current App Covers

Hydrodynamics

  • Analytical RTD generation for several standard families
  • Experimental RTD reconstruction from pulse, step, equation, or table data
  • Moment-based interpretation with overlays in E(t) and E(θ)

Reactive analysis

  • Segregation and Maximum Mixedness bounds
  • Ideal CSTR and Ideal PFR references
  • Equivalent-model routes through Tanks-in-Series and Dispersion Model
  • Design workspace with fitting, reactive comparison, and process optimization
  • Tracer-signal convolution and RTD estimation by deconvolution, including Tikhonov regularization
Useful mental model: Tab 1 defines the hydrodynamic description, Tabs 2-4 propagate that description through different reactive viewpoints, and Tab 5 organizes design decisions on top of those earlier layers. Tab 6 works with tracer signals and can return an estimated RTD to Tab 1.

Shared Assumptions You Should Keep in Mind

Assumption Meaning in practice
Internal SI calculations The app converts values when they are entered and displayed; internally it works in the International System of Units (SI).
RTD first, chemistry second Hydrodynamics are defined before the reactive models are applied. A poor RTD gives poor downstream predictions.
Isothermal routes The active tabs described here do not solve an energy balance.
Stoichiometry and kinetics must match Custom rate laws must be consistent with the stoichiometric matrix and species indexing.
Tab 5 optimization is process-side, not RTD editing The RTD stays fixed while inlet concentrations are varied inside the Optimization subtab.
Most common interpretation mistake: a nice numerical result does not automatically mean a physically correct answer. Check RTD quality, units, reaction definition, and whether the selected model assumptions really match the problem.

Shared Engineering Objects

RTD

The hydrodynamic object created in Tab 1. It stores the residence time distribution, moments, and family metadata used later by other tabs.

See RTD guide

ReactionSys

The chemistry object containing stoichiometry and kinetics. It is reused across Prediction, TIS, Dispersion, and Tab 5.

See object appendix

Stream

The inlet state object carrying composition and flow information. In the current Optimization workflow, inlet concentrations are the active decision variables.

See object appendix

Troubleshooting Map

Symptom Where to look first Most likely cause
No downstream tab can run Tab 1 The RTD was not generated, imported, or preserved as expected.
Prediction/TIS/Dispersion reports missing chemistry Tab 2 or imported source tab ReactionSys and/or Stream were not loaded into the selected path.
Imported experimental data behaves strangely Import appendix Column order, units, or C0 interpretation is inconsistent.
Dispersion open-open refuses to run Tab 4 guide The current kinetics are not supported by the exact open-open route.
Optimization gives a mathematically better result but not a useful one Optimization guide The target and constraints are screening-level and still depend on the quality of the chosen RTD and chemistry.
A restored session looks loaded but some plots or summaries are still blank Cross-tab workflow appendix The session restored state, but the current view may still need a fresh Compute to rebuild display-level results.
The estimated E has AE ≠ 1 Tab 6 guide The estimated RTD does not have unit area over the selected support; check baselines, tracer recovery, units, and the captured tail.
The deconvolution has a high relative residual Tab 6 guide The estimated RTD does not reconvolve closely enough to the measured outlet; recheck the signals, common grid, support, and regularization.
The estimated E is noisy or oscillatory Tab 6 guide Use Tikhonov regularization and compare the automatic L-curve choice with nearby manual λ values.
