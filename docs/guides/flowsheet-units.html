Configure Mixer, Splitter, Separator, and Heater

Each unit exposes only the independent information required by its material or energy balance.

Before You Start

Connect named Streams to every material port. Define thermodynamic data before using a Mixer energy balance or Heater/Cooler.

Mixer

Connect two or more inlet Streams and one outlet Stream. Add inlet and Remove change the number of material inputs. The outlet follows the combined material and energy balance.

VariablesRelations used
Outlet component flows, temperature, pressure, and volumetric flowKnown inlets determine the outlet by the material and energy balances; the Mixer needs no independent specification.
  1. Connect at least two inlet Streams and one outlet.
  2. Use Add inlet or Remove until the dialog matches the model.
  3. Apply; no independent process value is required.

Example: example 16, Problem 30 - Mixer and adiabatic CSTR, uses a Mixer upstream of a reactor.

Splitter

Connect one inlet Stream and two or more outlet Streams. Enter outlet fractions; one missing fraction is calculated automatically so the fractions sum to one. Every outlet retains the inlet state and composition.

VariablesRelations used
One fraction and total molar flow for each outlet, plus inlet total molar flowFractions sum to one; each outlet flow equals its fraction multiplied by inlet flow. A Splitter with three outlets therefore needs two independent specifications.
  1. Connect one inlet and all outlet Streams.
  2. Enter all but one outlet fraction, or specify an outlet flow in its Stream dialog.
  3. Leave one dependent fraction empty and apply.

Example: example 4, Parallel CSTRs (Problem 40b), uses flow splitting before parallel branches.

Separator

Connect one Feed, a Top Stream, and a Bottom Stream. Enter each component's Recovery to Top; the Bottom receives the remainder. This block is a component splitter, not an equilibrium flash.

VariablesRelations used
Recovery, Top and Bottom component flows, and Top and Bottom mole fractions for each componentRecovery divides each inlet component between Top and Bottom; each outlet composition follows its component flows. One independent value is needed per component.
  1. Connect Feed, Top, and Bottom Streams.
  2. Enter one recovery specification per component.
  3. Apply and check that no component row remains pink or red.

Example: example 49, CSTR, separator and recycle with purge, demonstrates both component recovery and recycle topology.

Heater / Cooler

Connect Feed to Product. Enter any one of outlet temperature, temperature change, or duty; the energy balance calculates the others. Enter either pressure drop or outlet pressure. Positive duty heats the stream, and thermodynamic data are required.

VariablesRelations used
Inlet and outlet temperature, temperature change, and dutyTemperature change links the two temperatures; the energy balance links outlet temperature and duty.
Inlet pressure, pressure drop, and outlet pressureOutlet pressure equals inlet pressure minus pressure drop.
  1. Connect Feed and Product Streams.
  2. Specify exactly one thermal target and one pressure relation.
  3. Apply and verify that dependent values become calculated.

Example: example 2, Adiabatic CSTR and cooler, uses the cooler to set the product temperature.

Frequent error: too many blue values over-specify a balance. Clear an independent entry so one field can be calculated.
