Configure CSTR, PFR, and Jacket blocks

Connect material ports first, then specify geometry, thermal behavior, pressure behavior, catalyst data, and active reactions.

Before You Start

Define chemistry and the inlet Stream. Add thermodynamic data for adiabatic or heat-exchange operation. A reactor uses a Feed material port, a Product material port, an optional Jacket signal, and an optional heat-duty output.

CSTR

Connect Feed to Product. Specify Volume, choose the thermal mode, and enter catalyst data only when the reactor is catalytic. Isothermal uses the inlet temperature, Adiabatic sets heat exchange to zero, and Heat exchange requires a connected Jacket. The optional heat output reports duty.

VariablesRelations used
Volume, residence time, space velocity, and inlet volumetric flowVolume equals residence time multiplied by inlet volumetric flow; space velocity is the inverse of residence time.
Catalytic option: catalyst mass, density, and porosityCatalyst mass follows occupied catalyst volume, density, and porosity.
  1. Connect Feed and Product Streams.
  2. Enter one independent size specification and choose the thermal mode.
  3. Enable catalytic operation only when needed and complete the exposed fields.
  4. Select the active reactions and press Apply.

Example: example 1, Isothermal CSTR, shows the simplest CSTR setup; example 2 adds an adiabatic energy balance and cooler.

PFR

Connect Feed to Product. Define geometry by Volume or Length; one of volume, length, diameter, and number of tubes is calculated from the other three. Choose constant pressure or provide the extra fluid and particle data requested for pressure drop. Thermal modes, catalyst settings, reaction selection, Jacket connection, and heat output work as in the CSTR.

VariablesRelations used
Volume, length, diameter, number of tubes, residence time, space velocity, superficial velocity, and inlet volumetric flowTube geometry determines volume; volume and inlet flow determine residence time; space velocity is its inverse; flow and tube area determine superficial velocity.
Catalytic and pressure-drop data when selectedCatalyst mass follows occupied catalyst volume. Non-constant pressure also uses the selected pressure-loss data.
  1. Connect Feed and Product Streams.
  2. Leave exactly one related geometry value for the dialog to calculate.
  3. Choose pressure and thermal modes and fill only the fields they expose.
  4. Select reactions, apply, and run.

Example: example 3, Adiabatic PFR, demonstrates axial reaction and temperature change.

Jacket

Connect the Jacket output to a reactor in Heat exchange mode. Utility (U*A) uses the heat-transfer coefficient, area, and utility inlet temperature; utility outlet temperature, heat capacity, condensation, and latent heat are available when needed. The other modes are Specified reactor T and Specified Q. Area and utility inlet temperature may also come from scalar input ports.

VariablesRelations used
Heat-transfer coefficient, area, their product, utility temperatures, heat capacity or latent heat, service flow, and reactor dutyThe coefficient multiplied by area gives conductance. After a Run, duty and the utility temperature change or latent heat determine service flow.
Specified reactor temperature or specified dutyThe selected mode needs one corresponding specification.
  1. Connect the Jacket signal to a reactor.
  2. Set that reactor to Heat exchange.
  3. Choose Utility, Specified reactor T, or Specified Q and complete only that mode's independent values.
  4. Apply both dialogs before running.

Example: example 29, Problem 34d - Jacketed Monsanto-reaction CSTR, uses a Jacket with a CSTR.

Frequent Errors

Check first: red fields indicate conflicting or invalid specifications. A heat-exchange reactor needs a connected Jacket, and non-isothermal modes need thermodynamic data.
