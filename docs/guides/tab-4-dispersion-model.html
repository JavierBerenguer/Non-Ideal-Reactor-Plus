Axial Dispersion

Tab 4 - Dispersion Model

This tab interprets the reactor through axial dispersion, usually via Pe or the equivalent internal dispersion number Bo = 1/Pe. It can use different boundary conditions and therefore must be read more carefully than Tabs 2 and 3.

Boundary Conditions Matter

Boundary mode Current role in the app
Closed-closedGeneral reactive route available in the current implementation.
Open-openAllowed only when the kinetics satisfy the current exact-support condition checked by the app.
Important: the app warns and stops if the chosen kinetics are not compatible with the exact open-open route. It does not silently switch to an alternative approximation.

When to Prefer This Tab

  • When you want to interpret the RTD with a dispersion parameter rather than with an integer-like cascade size.
  • When Peclet-number language is more natural for the problem you are studying.
  • When you want to compare the RTD and reactive behavior under explicit boundary assumptions.

Main Controls and Practical Meaning

Control or concept How to interpret it
Manual / From Calculated DataDetermines whether the dispersion route is driven by imported RTD information or by local parameter entry.
PeThe visible user-facing dispersion measure; large values approach plug-flow-like behavior.
Bo = 1/PeThe reciprocal internal representation used in the implementation.
Boundary selectorChanges not only the hydrodynamic interpretation but also which reactive route is admissible.
Chemistry loadingDefines whether the tab can compute reactive outlet and conversion sweeps at all.
Useful engineering reading: low Pe means stronger axial spreading and therefore behavior closer to backmixed limits; high Pe means sharper age separation and therefore behavior closer to plug flow.

For a direct comparison between Dispersion, Tanks-in-Series, and the full-RTD route, see the Model Selection guide.

Closed-closed in practice

This is the general reactive route currently available in the implementation and is the safer default when you want the dispersion interpretation without extra kinetic restrictions.

Open-open in practice

Use this only when the boundary assumption is truly relevant and the current kinetics satisfy the exact support condition enforced by the app.

If you need a refresher on the RTD side of the interpretation before judging the reactive differences, use the RTD Interpretation appendix.

Typical Questions to Ask Yourself

  • Does my problem call for open-open or closed-closed boundaries?
  • Are the kinetics compatible with the exact route currently implemented for open-open?
  • Am I interpreting a fitted equivalent model, or do I really want the full experimental RTD route from Tab 2?
Good habit: if the hydrodynamic conclusion changes strongly between TIS and Dispersion, revisit the RTD in Tab 1 and the direct full-RTD reading in Tab 2 before trusting either surrogate too quickly.

Typical Mistakes

  • Confusing the visible Pe input with the internal dispersion number Bo = 1/Pe.
  • Using open-open with unsupported kinetics and expecting the app to continue with a hidden approximation.
  • Interpreting a good dispersion fit as proof that the physical reactor really has one unique dispersion mechanism.
